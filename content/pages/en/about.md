## Why the union started

The union was created on 25 March 2026, out of a growing feeling of disconnect
between executives and employees.

That disconnect was confirmed a few days later, when the company announced
these changes to our working conditions:

- [10 of our PTO days would become "special days", taken away as soon as an
  employee hands in their resignation notice.](/en/demands/#what-we-can-and-will-discuss "See what we're doing about it")
- <span class="note" tabindex="0">Sick child leave would become unpaid. <span class="note-text" role="tooltip">Reversed</span></span>
- [The number of work-from-home days would be reduced for everyone.](/en/demands/#what-we-can-discuss-but-need-more-weight-behind "See what we're doing about it")

Since then, the union has been fighting to reverse these changes. So far the
company has reversed the sick child leave decision, explaining it as a
"miscommunication between the CEO and HR."

## What a union is, and how the law protects you

The right to form a union is in the Constitution.
[Article 28](https://www.japaneselawtranslation.go.jp/en/laws/view/174) says: "The right of workers to organize and to bargain
and act collectively is guaranteed." The details are in the
[Labor Union Act](https://www.japaneselawtranslation.go.jp/en/laws/view/3805/en).

### What counts as a union

Under Article 2 of the Labor Union Act, a union is an organization that workers
form voluntarily, mainly to maintain and improve their working conditions and
raise their economic status. It does not need the company's permission. It has
to stay independent from the company: it cannot be paid for by the employer, and
it cannot include supervisors with direct authority over hiring, firing or
promotions.

### What the company may not do

Article 7 lists what the law calls unfair labor practices. The company may not:

- dismiss you or treat you worse because you are a member, joined, tried to form
  a union, or took part in legitimate union activity;
- make not joining a union, or leaving one, a condition of your job;
- refuse to bargain with the union without legitimate grounds;
- control or interfere with how the union is formed or run, or fund it;
- treat you worse for filing a complaint with the Labor Relations Commission or
  giving evidence to it.

### Collective action is protected

Legitimate bargaining and union action cannot be treated as a crime
(Article 1.2), and the company cannot claim damages from the union or its
members for a legitimate strike (Article 8). Violence is never protected.

### If the company breaks these rules

A worker or the union can file a complaint with the Labor Relations Commission
of the prefecture, here the
[Tokyo Labor Relations Commission](https://www.toroui.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/lang/en/futougaiyou.html), within one year of what
happened (Article 27). If it finds an unfair labor practice, it can order the
company to put it right, for example to take someone back or to bargain.

## Union wins across Japan

- **Raises above 5%, three years running.** In the spring wage talks, unions in
  Rengo, Japan's largest federation, won average raises of 5.10% in 2024, 5.25%
  in 2025 and [5.01% in 2026](https://english.adnkronos.com/2026/07/03/japan-unions-clinch-5-01-pct-wage-hikes-in-2026-shunto/).
- **Equal treatment for contract staff at Japan Post.** Non-regular workers
  backed by their union took Japan Post to court. In October 2020 the Supreme
  Court ruled that denying them family allowances, year-end allowances, paid
  sick leave and summer and winter holidays was unreasonable and illegal
  ([Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2020/10/15/national/crime-legal/supreme-court-japan-post-nonregular/)).

### Tozen Union

[Tozen Union](https://tozenunion.org/) is a Tokyo union with many non-Japanese
members. Some of its wins:

- **Bargaining in English.** In August 2016 the Tokyo Labor Relations
  Commission ordered Tokyo Gakugei University to bargain with Tozen without
  insisting on Japanese or making the union bring an interpreter, and to
  apologize for refusing ([Tozen](https://tozenunion.org/tozen-union-wins-precedent-setting-negotiating-language-case-against-tokyo-gakugei-university/)).
- **Leafleting protected.** In January 2016 the commission ruled that the Japan
  College of Foreign Languages had illegally obstructed Tozen's leafleting, and
  ordered it to stop and to post an apology at the workplace for ten days. The
  national Central Labor Relations Commission upheld this in March 2018
  ([Tozen](https://tozenunion.org/national-labor-board-upholds-ruling-against-jcfl/)).
- **No refusing to negotiate.** In February 2025 the commission ruled that the
  Lycée Français International de Tokyo's refusal to negotiate with Tozen was an
  unfair labor practice, and ordered the school to promise in writing not to do
  it again ([Tozen](https://tozenunion.org/commission-orders-lycee-francais-to-negotiate-with-tozen/)).
- **Unpaid work has to be paid.** In November 2025 the Tokyo High Court upheld a
  ruling for a Tozen member at the same school: meetings and admin work outside
  his contracted hours counted as working time, and the school had to pay all
  outstanding wages and penalties ([Tozen](https://tozenunion.org/tozen-member-secures-landmark-victory-at-tokyo-high-court/)).

## Who we are

We are a union section made up of colleagues who work here. No one in the
section is paid by the company to do union work, and nothing we publish is
reviewed by management before it goes out.

## How we work

- **We check before we publish.** If we cannot confirm something, we say that it is unconfirmed.
- **We never speak in your name without asking.** What you tell the section stays with the section.
- **We decide together.** Positions are discussed in section meetings, which are open to members.

## Joining

Joining is a personal decision. Your membership can stay confidential, but it
helps us if we can show the company which departments are in the union, because
that gives us more bargaining power. Membership gives you a vote in the section's decisions and support from the wider federation, but you
do not need to be a member to ask us for help.

Write to the section to get the details.
