These are the demands the section submitted to the company on 30 October 2026,
together with a request for collective bargaining.

They are sorted by something that is not obvious from the outside: **whether we
can legally make the company sit down and discuss them with us.** That depends
on who is in the union, not on how good the demand is.

## What we can't discuss yet, but want to

> **We cannot bargain over this, because we have no members in the departments
> it affects.** A company is only obliged to negotiate with a union over the
> working conditions of the people that union represents. One colleague from
> the affected team joining changes that immediately.
>
> **[Join the union: contact us](mailto:cogentlabsunion@gmail.com)** ·
> Membership is confidential, and nothing is shared with management.

- **Restore flex-time for the Sales team.** Sales is currently required in the
  office at 8:30 every day. We have no Sales members, so the company has no
  obligation to discuss it with us.

  <div class="evidence">
  <details>
  <summary>Sources the union cites</summary>

  *From our bargaining request of 30 March 2026.*

  - Having to submit your schedule a week ahead turns flex-time into fixed hours.
  - It adds admin work for employees and for managers.
  - It makes it harder to deal with the unexpected, at home or at work, and makes
    people less willing to stay late when work needs it.
  - It adds nothing to the old system, where people already planned their hours
    around meetings and releases.
  - We asked to go back to the original flex-time, with core hours from 11:00 to
    16:00.
  - Legal basis: under the [Labor Contract Act, Articles 9 and 10](https://www.japaneselawtranslation.go.jp/en/laws/view/3744), working
    conditions cannot be changed to a worker's disadvantage without their
    agreement, unless the company shows the change is reasonable.

  </details>
  <details>
  <summary>Sources the company cites</summary>

  *From the company's written answer of 6 April 2026.*

  - The rule to submit a schedule a week ahead is already in the flex-time
    regulations, so it is not a new rule.
  - A week's notice is there to avoid disruption to operations, which the company
    considers reasonable.
  - Employees ticked a box confirming they had read the rules before using
    flex-time, so the company does not accept that this is a disadvantageous
    change or a move to fixed hours.

  </details>

## What we can discuss, but need more weight behind

We can put this on the table. Whether it moves depends on how many of us are
behind it.

- **More remote days, for everyone.** Our filed demand: keep one come-to-office
  day a week and let each person choose office or remote for the other days, on
  the premise of internal team adjustment and sharing with the manager.

  <div class="evidence">
  <details>
  <summary>Sources the union cites</summary>

  *From our bargaining request of 30 March 2026.*

  - **The company has to show a need, not a preference.** Under the
    [Labor Contract Act, Article 10](https://www.japaneselawtranslation.go.jp/en/laws/view/3744), it must show what concrete problem
    the current policy causes and how more office days would fix it.
  - **Telework rules should be agreed, not imposed.** The
    [MHLW telework guideline](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/content/tw_guideline.pdf) says scope, frequency and eligible days
    should be discussed and agreed by labor and management in advance.
  - **Hybrid work keeps people without hurting output.** A randomized trial of
    1,612 employees at Trip.com found hybrid work cut resignations by a third with
    no loss of performance, most of all for non-managers, women and people with
    long commutes ([Bloom, Han & Liang, Nature, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07500-2)).
  - **Remote work goes with productivity growth.** The US Bureau of Labor
    Statistics found a positive link between remote work and productivity growth
    across industries ([BLS, 2024](https://www.bls.gov/opub/btn/volume-13/remote-work-productivity.htm)).
  - **Commuting is a real cost.** Workers value hybrid work at about 8% of their
    salary, mostly from saving around 5 hours of commuting a week
    ([Bloom, IMF, 2024](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/fandd/issues/2024/09/working-from-home-is-powering-productivity-bloom)).
  - **Most employers are not going back.** Only 12% of companies with hybrid or
    remote staff planned a return-to-office mandate
    ([Stanford and Atlanta Fed, 2025](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2025/03/return-to-office-not-everybody-is-doing-it)).
  - **It matters for hiring in Japan.** 82.2% of Japanese workers want to keep
    teleworking ([WEF, 2025](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/12/navigating-work-transformation-in-japan/)). IT has the highest telework rate of any
    industry in Japan, at 56.2% (Persol Research Institute, 2024). Teleworkers in
    Japan earn 30 to 40% more once other factors are controlled for
    ([Morikawa, RIETI, 2025](https://www.rieti.go.jp/jp/publications/dp/25e001.html)).
  - **Attendance should not count as performance.** The MHLW guideline says rating
    people higher for coming in, rather than for their output, is not appropriate.
  - **Name the roles, don't apply a blanket rule.** Most of our work is done on a
    computer. If some tasks need presence, the company should identify them.
  - **Fix the process instead.** Coordination problems call for a shared
    collaboration day, clear meeting habits and proper onboarding, not more office
    days for everyone.
  - **Different rules need a reason.** Giving PMs one remote day and engineers two
    needs an objective justification.
  - **People relied on this policy.** Colleagues chose where to live, how to
    arrange childcare, and sometimes this job, based on it. Under Article 10, that
    reliance makes the disadvantage weigh more.
  - **Our request:** one fixed office day a week, with the other days decided by
    each person and their manager.

  </details>
  <details>
  <summary>Sources the company cites</summary>

  *From the company's written answer of 6 April 2026.*

  Adding Wednesday to the existing Monday and Friday office days is, the company
  says, about operational need and not about attendance for its own sake, given
  expected growth and a difficult business environment. It gives five reasons:

  - **Communication:** chance conversations and informal sharing that are harder
    remotely, building trust and team unity.
  - **Innovation:** more contact across teams and roles, leading to new ideas and
    process improvements.
  - **Training and retention:** more in-person on-the-job training and daily
    guidance for junior staff and mid-career hires.
  - **Culture and engagement:** a stronger sense of belonging from sharing the same
    space more often.
  - **Performance and clients:** current results and competition call for shared
    urgency. Three office days mean closer sharing of client issues and project
    status, and faster, better client response.

  </details>
  </div>

If you want this, agreeing quietly won't move it. Joining will, because it
makes the number across the table bigger.

## What we can and will discuss

These are on the agenda and we can hold the company to them.

- **Let people keep all their PTO days when they resign.** Anyone who resigns
  after 2 years of employment keeps every PTO day, including the 10 the company
  now calls special days.

  <div class="evidence">
  <details>
  <summary>Sources the union cites</summary>

  *From our bargaining request of 30 March 2026.*

  - Our employment contracts promise 20 days of paid leave. The new policy does
    not match what we agreed to.
  - A labor and social security attorney told us: if the written working
    conditions grant 20 days, the company cannot later redefine part of them as
    special leave that can expire. That takes away a right and is invalid.
  - The law only requires 2 weeks' notice. Losing 10 days the moment you give
    notice pushes people to resign as late as possible, which is worse for
    handovers and for hiring replacements.
  - Colleagues who stay on longer to help with a handover lose a benefit their
    contract promised them.
  - In March we asked to go back to 20 regular days with no restrictions.

  </details>
  <details>
  <summary>Sources the company cites</summary>

  *From the company's written answer of 6 April 2026.*

  - The change does not apply to leave already granted. No past days are turned
    into special leave.
  - Statutory paid leave is still granted as the law requires. The special days
    come on top of it.
  - Special days work like normal paid leave, except that they can't be used once
    you have given notice.
  - They exist so that people with short tenure get as much leave as long-tenure
    colleagues, to encourage them to stay and keep healthy. So the company limits
    them for people who are leaving.
  - The work rules require a handover on resignation (Article 81.1) and two more
    weeks of work when resigning for personal reasons (Article 75.4). The company
    says the restriction is consistent with that.
  - Special leave is a discretionary benefit, so limiting it is not in itself
    unlawful or invalid.

  </details>
  </div>

## Where this stands

We have asked the company for a collective bargaining session on **30 November
2026, 19:00 to 21:00**, in a meeting room at the company, with interpreters on
both sides.

**That date is our proposal, not a fixture.** The company has not answered yet,
so nothing about the time, the place or the session itself is settled. We will
say so here when it is.

Once the session happens we will publish what was agreed, refused, or left
open, the same way we publish everything else.
